# Confidence in historical evidence

Classify material claims by the evidence available, not by how plausible the code looks:

| Tier | Evidence and phrasing |
| --- | --- |
| Direct | An author, review, ticket, or design record explicitly states the reason. Attribute the statement and cite it: “The PR says this was added because…” |
| Supported | Multiple independent indirect observations converge. Cite them and say “The evidence points to…”. This remains a derived conclusion. |
| Inferred | A reasonable interpretation of limited context. State the observed facts, the inference, and uncertainty. |
| Speculative | A plausible hypothesis with thin support or equally plausible alternatives. Label it as a possibility. |
| Unknown | Available searches did not resolve the question. Name what was searched and what is missing. |

Do not infer author intent from naming, style, a test, or current behavior alone. A threshold change proves a value changed, not why that value was selected. Tests can establish intended edge cases, but not necessarily the business motivation.

A user's hypothesis is one candidate to test, not a conclusion to confirm. Preserve contradictions with citations and chronology. A later explanation may describe a new justification rather than the original motivation. Copied PR text in a ticket is one evidence lineage, not two independent confirmations.

Check whether a source states intent, reports an observed outcome, or merely proposes a plan. Do not convert “intended to fix” into “proved fixed”. Distinguish a historically true constraint from one still valid today. Missing search results do not establish that a discussion never occurred.

Read substantive sources beyond snippets and titles. Quote sparingly or paraphrase accurately with a precise pointer. If evidence cannot be opened, mark it unverified. Before delivery, every material claim has supporting evidence or a clear uncertainty label; no tidy narrative may override conflicting facts.
